## Is society facing an increase in depression?
- Prevalence for any mood disorder is a stable 6%, and depression itself 5.1-5.2%.
	- Done by the NEMESIS study (in the Netherlands)
	- Done in Australia as well
	- Bottom line: until 2010 stable, but in 2019-2022 (during covid) increase was 82%, at 10.8% (any mood disorder) and 9.3% (depression)
		- Very substantial increase, changes answer from NO to 'i don't know, we cannot ignore the figures'
		- Was it due to COVID?
			- They compare the first 1500 people (before COVID) to the later 1000
				- There was NO DIFFERENCE
		- And, the follow up from the first 2 parts of the NEMESIS study did a follow-up a few years later, and they show the same 
		- Could it also be that the new generation is more open to talk about symptoms, which is why they score higher on depression, and therefore have fewer severe cases
			- No, younger people actually require more care
	- Indications have been found that anxiety increased as well
- Elephants in the room:
	- Social media use has been linked to mental health issues
		- But its very difficult to answer the question if it actually is relevant
		- Correlation is NOT causation
- Outcomes of depression:
	- during covid no rise in suicide rates, but it could be similar to 9/11, where in the US suicide went down
		- Collective traumatic events cause social cohesion
	- Suicides have not gone up, but we did not register it 20 years ago
	- Suicides are also very hard to measure
- Scandinavian countries are very good at following depression, and therefore epidemiologists have a good area to do studies
	- As a citizen you are automatically enrolled in studies, have to opt out specifically
	- These data also show that there is a *big increase* in mood disorders the last few years
	- *men have lower incidence than women*
	- *first peak is in the first part of the lifespan*
## Depression paradox
- Why are we not having a decrease on depression?
	- There is more and more money spent on mental healthcare, more and more people on antidepressants
- Why?
	- Well, antidepressants are given to many more diagnoses than just depression
	- Should we stop antidepressants? For how long should you be treated?
		- A lot of chronic users of antidepressants
- Is adequate care being given?
	- about 50 % of patients in wealthy countries
	- way worse in low/middle income countries
	- Prevalence in high/low income countries is the same
		- is treatment not effective?
## Vicious cycle betweeen depression and somatic disease
- People who are depressed often move less, smoke more, drink more, sport less
	- Causes somatic disease / physical health
	- Difference in lifestyles
	- But even accounted for this (using covariates), physical disease is increased
- So, there might be a biological link between depression and somatic health
## Balance between vulnerability and environment
- Nature / nurture
	- Party life experiences, partly genetic vulnerability
- Genetic basis of depression:
	- Heritability is about 37%, much lower than other mental conditions
		- Schizophrenia, addictive behaviors, etc.
	- Depression is consistently lower, so environment must play an important role
- Depression is a classic example of a polygenic disease, many hits
### Genetic background
- Is depression a brain disorder, not caused by society?
	- Looking at the SNPs in depressed people, a lot of genes that have to do with the brain show up
	- Thus, very likely that it is indeed a disorder of the brain
	- But, a lot of environmental forces as well
### Biological systems
- Very much aligned with a study recently published (by the teacher)
	- Involve both central and peripheral processes
		- stress-related systems that we have (immune, HPA axis)
		- summary: 
			- Structural: lower hippocampus, amygdala, prefrontal cortex (small effect sizes)
			- Connectivity: higher DMN, lower salience & central executive networks, both lower and higher fronto-striatal-limbic FC
			- Functional activation: a lot more to negative, less activation to positive stimuli, striatum, prefrontal cortex
### Stress systems
- Autonomic nervous system activated 
	- Causes chronic stress, not good for body -> stress that is normally good becomes bad if it is always activated
- HPA axis also involved, creates cortisol
- Immune system also more involved
- Hypothesis: People with depression are constantly more stressed, have more stress activation
	- These are also linked with somatic disease
### Depression & autonomic nervous system
- compared heart rate & heart rate variability between depressed and healthy controls
	- Depressed people have higher heart rate, lower heart rate variability
		- But, this is only the case of people using SSRIs
	- Then they looked at longitudinal analysis, and the ones who were switching from non-medication to medication and vise versa showed differences in heart rate
	- More evidence!! Medication might be relevant for cardiovascular function
### Depression & HPA axis
- Creates cortisol
	- But, very hard to measure because of the white coat effect
	- Blood drawing is a stressful event! Since cortisol rises after 15 minutes, you will never test baseline cortisol
- Looking at tit by saliva: people with MDD are more susceptible to higher cortisol after stress
- Looking at it by hair: More chronic way of looking at it
	- Also, people with MDD have higher cortisol when more severely depressed
### Depression & immune system
- Also correlated, looking at blood and genetic 
	- Over-expression of genes relevant to immune system in people with MDD
- Anti-inflammatory treatment of depression?
	- anti-inflammatory medication seems *a bit of a clinical effect*
	- Studies were not designed to test this
	- A clinical trail found that it had no effect
