## MS pathology
-  Demyelination -> by protein PLP
- Leukocyte infiltration -> MHC class II
- Foamy macrophages -> foamy due to eating up myelin
- Perivascular infiltrate -> blood cells escape the cell stream and end up in perivascular space
## Grey matter pathology: Luxol fast blue staining
- Neglected research area due to problems with visualizing GM lesions using conventional histochemical staining procedures
	- They are harder to see, but grey matter structures also show demyelination
## Cortical MS lesions
- Found most frequently
- 4 different types:
	1. Type I -> lesions spanning both grey and white matter
		- Called Leukocortical (white & cortex)
	2. Type II lesions: entirely intracortical
		- Often begins surrounding a blood vessel
		- Pretty small, hard to detect with MRI
	3. Type III -> Loss of myelin near meninges
		- Not visible without myelin antibody staining
	4. Type IV -> dymeylination of entire cortex, but stops at white matter
		- weird......... (will type III also stop at the white matter)
## How does a person get MS?
-  New theory, outside in model: Something happens in Immune system first, which then affects the CNS
	- In MS, t-cells get a little bit confused causing killer t-cells to start eating myelin
	- Happens if the BBB is open
	- Normally, leukocytes have specific molecules on their surface, allowing them to find their way into the central nervous system
- Other theory: Inside out model -> something happens in the brain first, causing immune system to respond
	- Could be caused by many different things that cause subtle damage
# Specific causes (there are many many more)
## Viral Infection
- This is been thought for a long time
- Epstein-Barr virus in recent years
	- Nearly all people who develop ms have had this virus (necessary, not sufficient)
	- But, 96% of people have had it, so does it really explain it? Maybe not
## Oligodendrocyte damage
- Animal model with oligodendrocyte cell death was found not to cause demyelination
	- So outside in model?
- Well, no. If they waited, they were able to induce MS-like symptoms by starting in the brain
## Disrupted axo-myelinic synapse -> pathogenetic triggers
- inner myelin sheet near the axon communicates just like two neurons
	- Using ion channels, etc
	- If something goes wrong here, this could lead to demylination
- It was found that this results in blisters characteristic of MS
