# Leukodystrophies: envision gene therapy
## Genetic mutations causing disease
- caused by repeat expansions, deletions, or point mutations
	- This causes protein dysfunction
- Toxic gain of function -> not gonna be talked about today
## Gene therapy: What is it?
- Manipulate the genome of a patient to correct a broken gene
- Relevance of developing gene therapy:
	- 7.500 inherited diseases identified, 40% of the genetic defect has been identified
	- Gene therapy could provide potential life-long cure!
### Vector types used for monogenic disorders
- Single gene defect -> easier to target
- A *virus* could be used for this. 
	- *Lentivirus*  -> in vivo
	- *AAV* based -> retroviral vectors (ex vivo)
		- Transductation
		- Adeno-associated virus
## Gene therapy for leukodystrophies
### AAV vectors
- Serotypes for targeting specific tissues / cells
- Promotors for general or specific expression
- Transgene
- how to delier it to the brain?
	- CSF, intra-cerebellar ventricular injection, all invasive
	- Non-Invasive:
		- Non-replicating AAV9
			- Also used for commercial treatments already
			- You target the neurons, express survival mode in neurons
			- Intravenous injection but crosses the barrier! -> under the age of 2
			- Normally used for spinal muscular atrophy
			- Has a risk, can be liver-toxic. -> *hepatotoxicity*
		- Cytomegalovirus
- It does still go to the brain and *CSF* -> using capsid engineering we can find which capsids are best for it to go to the CSF
	- _capsids_ are the protein shells that encase and protect the viral genetic material. The capsid plays a critical role in the virus’s ability to infect cells and deliver therapeutic genes in gene therapy applications.
	- Can help to select which cells it will target!
- Using this, gene therapy ameliorates clinical symptoms of VWM
### Limitations of capsid engineering
- You do it in mice, but since the way it works is very strain specific, translating to a human setting is a lot more difficult
	- Human viruses different than human viruses
	- Even slight differences in strains can lead to big problems or no penetrance
### Disadvantages of AAV
- CNS biodistibution 
- High vector concentrations in injection sites
## Ex vivo gene therapy
- Gammaretroviral vectors -> used in history
- Lentiviral vectors -> based on HIV
- Apharetroviral vectors
### What is the difference between AAV and retroviral viruses
- retroviralvectors are less stable, as they are enveloped
- they are also based on RNA
- But, once they are integrated, you cannot get rid of them
- Often used hematopoiesis
	- Uses microglia-like cells, which can go to the brain and target disease there
- What is the main disadvantage of using this approach compared to AAV?
	- Takes *time* -> 
		- Taking stem cells out, manipulating them, and growing them takes a long time
		-  When you re-infuse the cells, it takes time to get to the microglia cells that affect the brain
		- They also become infertile. 
- Based On the principle of lysosomal trafficking
### HSC gene therapy
- HIV based lentiviral vectors -> efficient and safe
- cell engraftment in the brain -> conditioning
- Restricted expression possible -> promoters
	- Everyone uses this initially in trails to see if you get an effect
- Considerations:
	- Can you get sufficient therapeutic protein produced, secreted, and taken up sufficiently for gene therapy to be effective?
	- Do sufficient numbers of gene modified microglia enter the CNS
	- Transgene product toxic to HSC
- Sometimes it can lead to genotoxicity -> could lead to tumors
