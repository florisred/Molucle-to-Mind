#### Classic VWM
- Early onset age, 1-6 years. 
- Chronic motor decline, less obvious cognitive decline, mild epilepsy
- Episodes of rapid severe motor decline following fever or common cold, For example
	- Friday, child is tired
	- Saturday, child is hysterical
	- Sunday, child is in coma
	- Afterwards, sometimes recovery, but often death
#### Early onset VWM
- Onset before birth or in 1st year
- No real development, fast regression
- Involvement of other organs than the brain
- Death within months
#### Late onset VWM
- Onset in adulthood
- Cognitive decline, later less striking motor decline
- Slow disease progression, fewer stress-provoked episodes of rapid decline
- Death after decades
#### Genetic Cause
- Mutations in EIF2B1 - EIF2B5
- Encode alpha-eta subunits
- Function of EIF2B
	- is conditional for translation of all mRNAs into proteins and essential for down-regulating mRNA translation / protein production under stress
		- If it is very active, there is a lot of protein synthesis
		- If it is inactive, there is no protein synthesis
	- Mutations in the gene decrease EIF2B activity, patients only have minor mutations, never 2 null mutations
##### Integrated stress response (ISR)
- This is the stress response of cells
	- Stressors include infection, misfolded protein, amino acid starvation, heat, etc.
	- Stress response goes through EIF2B
- Causes protein synthesis to go down (because it takes a lot of energy)
- Activate transcription response -> 
	- many different factors, are going to determine whether cell dies or recovers
	- If there is a lot of stress for a long time, the cell will undergo *apoptosis*
- If people have VWM, there is enough EIF2B for protein synthesis
	- However, if there is a stress response, disease starts
	- Causal, bc the more stress the higher the VWM disease
#### Treatment of VWM
- While we do not know everything or even a lot about the disease, we still want to treat it!
- What we can target:
	1. Reduce stress response upstream of EIF2B
		- Causes fewer cases of acute rapid decline
		- Chronic decline still there (not targeted)
	2. Increase EIF2B using EIF2B activators
		  - There was a drug called Antihypertensive drug, which was already FDA approved, but no longer used
		  - It was known that it impacted the ISR, activating EIF2B indirectly
		  - Using mouse models and knockouts, it was seen that it works about 80%
	- Clinical trail:
		- easier BC it was already FDA approved

## What is a leukodystrophy
- Genetic, progressive disorders primarily affecting myelin, either directly or through oligodendrocytes
- In the 1980s, there were no known gene defects
	- MRI had not entered clinical practice, so only a biochemical analysis of brain tissue and data from enzymatic and metabolic defects were sources of data
	- Curative treatment would of course only focus on myelin
- When MRI was introduced, it was the first time white matter abnormalities could be seen with high sensitivity
- It replaced neuropathology
- Using MRI pattern recognition and better DNA sequencing it was discovered that leukodystrophies were mostly genetic defects
- new definition of leukodystrophy (2016)
	- Genetic white matter disorder due to a defect in any of the white matter structural components
### A new classification of leukodystrophies
- Oligodendrocyte and myelin disorders
- Astrocytopathies
- Leuko-axonopathies
- Leuko-microgliopathies
- Leukovasculopahties

## VWM is caused by pathogenic variants in EIF2B
### Disease mechanism
- Literature search, is there anything known about EIIF2B?
	- Yes, it binds to the initiator tRNA<sup>MET</sup> within the ternary complex component of the preinitiation comlex
	- eIF2B is essential for all cells, housekeeping & protein synthesis
	- DNA -> transcription -> mRNA -> translation -> Pretein
	- mRNA has a 5' *cap* (protects against decay and degredation) 
	- Translation:
		- Initiation phase: Before it begins
		- Elongation phase: elongation of the protein
		- Termination phase: Last, where it stops
	- Translation initiation: 
		- eIF2 has a TGPT attached (active molecule) with a Met attached
		- Binds to 43S -> then has a high affinity for the *cap*, and starts the scanning phase
			- Scans until it encounters an *AUG* (start) codon. 
			- then, eIF2 is released and GTP is turned into GDP, and met is released
			- Translation goes to *elongation* phase
		- You then need something to restart translation -> eIF2B rephosphilates the eIF2-GTP!
			- the cycle can start again!
	- In this way, eIF2B regulates protein synthesis rate
		- If there is less eIF2B, the cycle goes slower, so there is *less translation*
		- This happens for example during *protein stress* [[HC2 Clinical Neuroscience, Leukeodystrophies#Integrated stress response (ISR)|Integrated stress response]]
		- Stress causes eIF2 to be phosphorylated (done by kinases)
			- when that binds to eIF2B, GADD34, CHOP, and ATF4 are synthesized
			- is a quick and adaptive response, that maintains proteostasis (the balance of proteins (it only slows it down) and saves energy)
			- transcriptome changes -> the translation machinery becomes available for translation different set of mRNAs
			- Stress-amelioration -> cells may suffer from too much stress and go into apoptosis
	- So, a more complete picture: sensors (kinases) sense stress, and slow down protein synthesis
### How do mutations in eIF2B then cause VWM?
- Mouse model 
	- Onset of ataxia at about 4 months of age in mice
	- Caused a reduced life span
	- Brain white matter: less myelin, white matter, etc
- So then, they did an open screen 
	- Polysomal profiling -> looking at different mRNAs that are differentially expressed
	- Looked at *before* the disease was there, but *after* some white matter changes have happened
	- Interestingly, many of the genes they tested *increased* in activity (not what was expected)
		- You expect translation to go down with problems in eIF2B
		- It was seen that ATF4 and CHOP (at least in mouse brain) increase the cascade of translation
	- Then they saw that phosphorylation was *reduced*
		- So the mutation itself reduces eIF2B, and sets in motion the whole cascade
