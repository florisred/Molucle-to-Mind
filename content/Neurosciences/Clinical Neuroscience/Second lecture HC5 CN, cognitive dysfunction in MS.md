
## Cognitive dysfunction & assessment of it
- First some basics of MS, we have seen this already a thousand times
- While most symptoms are sensory /  measurable, a lot of them are *invisible*
	- Depression, fatigue, pain, bowel / bladder, and *cognition*
- *Cognition* is one of the most frequent symptoms of MS
	- 40%-60% 
	- *Appropriate controls*
		- if you have certain population with certain demogrpahic characterstics, if youre not comparing them to appropriate controls you will have an unfair comparison
		- 60 yo to 20 yo is unfair, deficits might be due to wrong control group
- Most people have at least 1 domain cognitive deficits, but many also 2-3 or even more
	- PPMS is most prevalent
### Why is it important?
- Cognitive impairments make it harder to get by day to day
- Harder to get a job
- Harder to work with friends, keep appointments, etc
### Main cognitive functions affected
- *IPS*: information processing speed
- *Memory*: Verbal + executive
- Executive function
- *Attention*
### Cognitive assessment
- There is a specific battery for cognitive function in MS
- The mechanisms in play between RRMS and PPMS might be different
	- Therefore it can be important to sort results in phenotype
	- Progressive patients often have higher cognitive defects
- Self report:
	- The will report feeling slower, forget more, keeping attention on things
	- We try to have a look at these complaints (*patient reported outcome, subjective*)
		- Try to look at the association with *objective tests*
	- There is a big *dissociation* between the complaints and the objective testing
		- However, they all correlate with *depression score*
			- Not known, we need longitudinal studies with depression scores as *primary outcomes*
			- Since all of these studies are *in vivo*, inferring causation is not really possible
#### MSNQ - Multiple Sclerosis Neuropsychological Screening Questionnaire
- If you give the same questionnaire to people close to the patients, the results correlate *way* more with objective assessment than the patient reported ones
	- Interesting
### Cognitive impairment at early stages of RRMS: IPS impairment
- Since early years, we can now basically diagnose *CIS* as clinical MS
	- Used to be different
- But, in early patients we can already test *information processing speed*
	- Why? -> main domain affected in MS, and very easily assessed
- Increases in impairment after first MS phase are stark
- *Radiologically isolated syndrome:*
	- Someone has an unrelated MRI, and we discover MS lesions
	- If you follow these patients, they can convert to Clinical MS
	- We keep monitoring them
	- Cognitive dysfunction has already been seen here
#### How do you detect cognitive dysfunction at early stages?
- Do tests, then look at Z-score of both clinical and healthy people
	- If there is a -1.5, that could be indicative of dysfunction
		- Especially if corroborated by other domains
	- 1.5 is not very static, depends on authors and choices made.
- You have to have a very large group of healthy controls, very diverse, so you can match with the right group. 
#### IPS
- Information processing speed
- Has an impact on basically *all other cognitive domains*
	- So, it is very hard to measure, and it is hard to separate one from the other
### Short batteries
- Idea is that you have the full neuropsychological battery, and you make this battery smaller by picking one from most subareas for tests
- Useful, because cognitive tests are hard to administer because they take *so much time*
	- Therefore, shorter batteries (restricted to essential tests) are important
	- Yes, you will miss *something*, but better than not doing it at all
- Sometimes shortened from 4 hours to 15 minutes
	- A lot more feasible
### Practice effect
- If you administer the test to people multiple times, they have an advantage
	- Causes a *practice effect problem*, which we have to consider
- Solution: *different forms*
	- If you change the key for the IPS test for example, it could be helpful
- Problem:
	- Practice effect might be *different* between MS patients and healthy folk
### Ecological assessment
- A virtual reality environment that mimics real life
	- Tests attention, executive function, and memory
- For example with a green light test or something
### Summary
- We have a predictable cognitive *footprint* of MS in large studies
- Change over time is usually slow and subtle, so it is difficult to see if people are decreasing in terms of performance
	- But, decline is there! 
	- Can be seen with Z-scores if you compare with normal people
	- Also if you a decrease of 8 points, you know there is clinical decline
- Patients with progressive disease tend to have more widespread and more severe cognitive impairment 
- Cognition is related to other disease factors and can predict disease progression
