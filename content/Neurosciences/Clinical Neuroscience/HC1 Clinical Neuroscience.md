## Head size
- Head size is determined by the volume inside the skull
	- Brain & CSF
- The brain grows to 80% of its adult volume during the first 2 years
	- A bigger or smaller head can thus be an indication
		- Macrocephaly -> Bigger head: *hydrocephalus or brain tumor*
## Development and milestones
### Sensory-motor
- *Gross motor skills* as a result of myelination of motor neurons
- Before milestones
	- *Muscle tone*: how the child lays
	- You often look for *asymetry* in tone / movement
		- Put the head in the middle, otherwise the *primitive reflexes* interfere
			- Suckling reflex
			- Disappear after 6 months of life, but primitive reflexes appear again in dementia if cortical functions disappear
- *Motor milestones*
	- 3 months: 
		- kid can turn around and hold itself on chest
	- 1 year: 
		- Walking
		- Pinching / grabbing
	- There are a lot of individual differences between kids, but there will always be a *limit for what is normal*
### Communication
- First things: *smile* at the age of three months
- Language comprehension
	- *after 2 years*: 25 words
	- *In third yea*r: combination of words
### Social-emotional
- Emotions, sharing joy, not wanting to be left alone
### Cognitive
- Executive function, more abstract
- Imitation
- Symbolic play -> feeding a doll
## Psychiatric and neurological disorders have discrete ages of onset

- You don't diagnose schizophrenia in children aged 8 for example, as that normally  happens after 15 years

## Examples of problems in neural development
### Neurulation
- Inductive events that occur on the dorsal aspect of the embryo and results in the formation of the brain and spinal cord.
	- Regulated by very specialized developmental processes
- Ectoderm: Outermost tissue layer of the embryo
	- Forms brain & spinal chord
	- Follows *grastulation*
	- Cells migrating to the interior of the embryo making 3 layers: Endoderm, mesoderm, ectoderm
- After that, ectoderm is told to make neural plate -> then folds in on itself creating neural tube
	- This is what CNS is! 
	- Ectoderm creates both skin & neural cells, thats why they are so alike 
- Then forms alar plate & basal plate
	- Alar plate forms cerebral hemispheres, basal plate the diencephalon
### Spina Bifida & neurofibromatosis
- Some neurological diseases can be diagnosed when you look to the skin
	- These cells and neurons are related through the differentiation process
	- Causes neural tissue in the back to disfunction
		- *Paralysis*, *muscle weakness*
		- *Insensitive skin* -> loss of sensation
		- bladder & bowel disfunction

- Not as prevalent anymore due to more *folic acid* in the diet
- Neurofibromatisis causes spots on the skin -> more than six can indicate neural problems and access growth

### Neuronal migration
- The precursor neurons come from an area which is close to the ventricles
	- They have to move and pass all the already-formed neurons to go to outside
	- They move along the *[[Glial cells]]*
- *Radial process*: from inside to outside
- Things that cause symptoms: *epilepsy*
	- Thick cortex without gyri
	- Sometimes you see in *MRI Flair* cortical cells near the ventricles -> these did not migrate well
### Germinal matrix
- You see the ventricular zone in an MRI in a 24 week old children
- You sometimes see some *bleeding*
### Neonatal gyration
- Around 24 weeks post-conception gyration starts
	- It then happens very rapidly
- Problems with gyration:
	- Smooth cortical surface: *lisencephaly*
	- too many gyri: *polymicrogyria* (cauliflower)
### CNS synaptogensis
- Activity dependent
### Myelination
- *Brain stem* is first myelinated -> vital functions
- *Motor functions* follow shortly after -> also very important
## Neonatal neuropathology
- Neonatal brain is very vulnerable for pathology
- Stationary defects
	- Congenital structural abnormabilites
	- Acquired problems due to hypoxia for example
- Progressive defects
- *Silberreis et al., The cellular and molecular landscapes of the developing human central nervous system (2016)* is a good resource for all of this
