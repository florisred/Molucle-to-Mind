# All diseases overview
## Spina Bifida
- Problem with neural tube formation, closure defect of the skin
	- What causes it? -> lack of folic acid
- Three types:
	- Occulta: Skin closed, defect in vertebral column
	- Meningocele: open defect (aperta)
	- Myelomeningocele (disfunctional neural tissue in cele)
- Can cause hydrocephalus
## Neurofibromatosis
- Cafe au lait spots, more than 6 is indication of neural problems
- NF1 = tumor supression gene, mutated
- Children have cerebral overgrowth
## MLC -> megalencephalic leukoencephalopathy with subcortical cysts
- Macrocephaly
- Mutations in MLC1, GLIALCAM, GPRC6B or AQP4 cause problems with astrocytic endfeet
	- Problem with brain, ion, water homeostasis
- causes chronic white matter edema, structure intact
## VWM -> Vanishing white matter
- Classic (1-6y, ataxia), early onset (<1y, no dev), late onset (>18y, more cognitive)
- Caused by eIF2Bq
	- eIF2Bq exchanges eIF2-GDP for eIF2-GTP, which is essential for all protein synthesis
	- eIF2-GTP is turned into eIF2-GDP after usage, so translation suppression if it doesnt work well
	- Normally enough, but during stressful events ISR is activated, reducing further eIF2B activity
	- Too little protein suppression for highly metabolic tissue such as white matter, dies.
## X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy
- Mutation in ABCD1 gene, problem with defective degeneration of VLCFAs
- Rapidly progressing white matter disease
- Also problem with adrenal failure (kidney)
## MLD (metachromatic leukodystrophy)
- Problem with lysosomal storage
- Low activity of ASA -> digests sulfatides
- too many sulfatides -> accumulate in membrane -> myelin first affected
- causes macrophages eating myelin to blow up bc cant digest sulfatides
- Axonal degeneration
## PMD -> pelizaeus merzbacher disease:
- PLP1 mutation (important myelin protein)
- Severe forms: connatal MPD -> no motor milestones
- Milder forms: mild PMD, normal cognition, walking with support
- Mildest form: x-linked spastic paraplegia
## MS
- RRMS, SPMS, PPMS, CIS
- Lesions in white & and gray matter
- autoimmune disease
- RAW: relapse associated worsening
- PIRA: Progression independent of relapse activity
- Diagnosis:
	- DIS, DIT
		- periventricular, (juxta)cortical, optic nerve, infratentorial, spinal cord
	- T1 black hole lesions: dead old tissue, severe axonal damage
	- T1 contrast: active lesions
	- T2 lesions: demyelinating lesions
	- Optical Coherence Tomography
- Leukocyte infiltration of brain, transforms into foamy macrophages (due to lipids)
- B/T cells end up in perivascular space close to blood vessels
- Also partly grey matter loss
- Outside in model (immune system confused and starts attacking brain), inside out model (first axonal damage, then myelin in lymph nodes -> immune system starts reacting to it)
- Myelin blisters hallmark of MS
- Remyelination fixes blisters
- Cognitive effects:
	- depression, fatigue, pain, blowel / badder, cognition
	- Self report for depression better, other cognitive better if family does it
## PD
- Diagnosis:
	- 1. establish parkinsonism -> bradykynesia + rigidity/resting tremor
		- resting Tremor: when movement no tremor, otherwise 3-6hz
			- Parkinsonian tremor:
				- Rest tremor, assymetrical, worse during mental task, re-emergent
		- Action tremor: during movement
		- Postural tremor: During maintenance of position
	- 2. is parkinsonism attributable to PD
		- Essential tremor: Not pd, but a familiar action tremor
		- Exclusion criteria: 
			- *MSA* -> cerebellar abnomralities, cross-bun sign
			- *PSP* -> downward vertical gaze palsy (cannot look down), hummingbird
			- *BFD* -> dementia
			- *Lower limb parkinsonism* -> not of upper body (probably vascular)
			- No l-dopa response
		- Red flags:
			- Rapid progression of gait impairment (normally slower, not always)
			- No motor symptom progression
			- Early bulbar dysfunction -> MSA?
			- Recurrent Falls
			- Absence of non-motor symptoms
			- Pyramidal tract signs
			- Symmetric parkinsonism
- Alpha-synuclein aggregates forming lewy bodies in substantia nigra, degeneration of dopaminergic neurons (also noradrenergic, serotonergic, and cholinergic problems)
- Non-motor symptoms:
	- depression, executive function, psychosis, impulse control disorders
	- Insomnia, REM sleep behavior disorder
	- Orthostatic hypotension, obstipation, incontinence, impotence
	- Olfactory loss (90%), pain, tiredness
- Imaging:
	- PET/SPECT display metabolic or biochemical activity, work with radiotracers
		- Dopamine, glucose metabolism, ceebral perfusion, pathological proteins
	- In PD: I-FP-CIT tracer, DA transporter
- Cortico-striatal-thalamocortical (CSTC) circuits
	- 3-5 parallel segregated circuits
		- motor cortex -> posterior putamen -> motor functions
		- DLPFC -> dorsal caudate -> associative functions
		- Obitofrontol cortex -> nucleus accumbens -> limbic
	- Direct pathway: gass
	- Indirect: brake, through de-activation of thalamus
		- D1 receptors activated by dopamine
		- D2 receptors inhibit by dopamine -> these are tonically active in PD, too much brake
- Pattern in many measures:
	- Corticospinal tract improves in the beginning, is a sign of cortical plasticity & compensation
	- But slowly, everything gets worse
- Braak lewy body stadia:
	- 1-2 -> lower bain stem + olfactory bulb
	-  3-4 -> lymbic regions
	- 5-6 -> neocortical regions
