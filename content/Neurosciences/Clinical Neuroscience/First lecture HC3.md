## Electricity in the brain & seizures
- To keep the membrane potential, you need [[Active transporters]]
	- Then you get storage of energy in the brain by uneven potentials
- In theory, the amount of flow of ions during electrical brain activity is quite small with one action potential, but if there are a lot of action potentials at the same time there might be a larger disruption
- only 10% of the brain is extracellular space
	- Easier to get buildup of ions there, especially glutamate and K<sup>+</sup>
		- If you dont manage this well, you get an epileptic seizure
- You can model this in a brain cell splice
	- If there is too much extracellular K<sup>+</sup>, the neuron can no longer fire action potentials
		- You see it as a very prominent depression of the extracellular space
		- This is what happens during a *migraine*
### Astrocytes are great at dealing with ions
- Astrocytes deal with ions well because they are inter-connected using gap junctions
	- Can both actively and passively pump in K<sup>+</sup>, which helps balancing
	- Also very well connected with the vasculature, and have pumps there. 
		- Since they are at the perivascular space, they can store and dump excess ions there
	- Potassium builds up underneath the myelin in axons, and spreads into the astrocyte network there too
		- If this does not happen, fluid will build up under the myelin
#### Takeup of potassium in astrocytes causes swelling
- Potassium is an osmotic particle
- If they take up extra potassium, they take up osmotic particles -> water will go along -> you get swelling
- Swelling is something the brain really should not do
	- Luckily, there are good cellular mechanisms to prevent it, called *Regulatory Volume Decrease*
		- Allows particles to flow out to decrease osmosis -> smaller volume
	- We don't really know how cells know they are swollen, but it opens during swelling
		- Could be *mechanosensitive*, sense that they are pulled away due to swelling
#### RVD is defective in white blood cells from MLC patients
- Lymphoblasts can trigger RVD if you give them an osmotic shock
	- In MLC patients this is not the case! -> would be problematic
- Mlc1-null mouse and Glialcam-null mice work very well for a neurological model of MLC
	- While they do not have the same motor problems, their neuro-pathology is the same
	- Swollen, a lot of extracellular water, and white matter is full of vacuoles
#### Epilepsy in MLC mice
- These MLC mice have a propensity for seizures
	- They go into seizures *much easier*
- if we isolate astrocytes, we can again see that their volume regulation is impaired
- Extracellular K<sup>+</sup> increased in MLC mice -> problem with potassium reuptake
	- Again linked to volume regulation defects
### Conclusions
- Loss of function of the astrocyte protein MLC1 causes a leukodystrophy
	- 76% have MLC1 defects
		- Even in patients with the same genetics, there is a spectrum of phenotypes
			- So, there is more to it than just this monogenic aspect
			- There are also some patients with issues with the promotor, that lead to a little bit of loss but not complete loss. They still have some sort of remittance
	- Others have the Glialcam
		- If you have a chromosomal deletion with Glialcam on it, you get Jacobsen Syndrome
			- Parents of these children do not have MLC
	- 2% have no genetic mutation
		- 
- White matter is present -> myelination happens, but swollen and more and more vacuoles
- Astrocyte endfeet appear chronically swollen
- MLC patients and mouse models show a seizure phenotype
- Due to problems with volume regulation of astrocytes
- MLC is genetically heterogeneous
- Differences in genetics only partially explain differences in phenotypes
- Recently, 2 new genes were linked to MLC in very rare patients
