## What is a leukodystrophy
- Genetic, progressive disorders primarily affecting myelin, either directly or through oligodendrocytes
- In the 1980s, there were no known gene defects
	- MRI had not entered clinical practice, so only a biochemical analysis of brain tissue and data from enzymatic and metabolic defects were sources of data
	- Curative treatment would of course only focus on myelin
- When MRI was introduced, it was the first time white matter abnormalities could be seen with high sensitivity
- It replaced neuropathology
- Using MRI pattern recognition and better DNA sequencing it was discovered that leukodystrophies were mostly genetic defects
- new definition of leukodystrophy (2016)
	- Genetic white matter disorder due to a defect in any of the white matter structural components
### A new classification of leukodystrophies
- Oligodendrocyte and myelin disorders
- Astrocytopathies
- Leuko-axonopathies
- Leuko-microgliopathies
- Leukovasculopahties
### Example: Hypomyelination with atrophy of basal ganglia and cerebellum
- Causes:
	- Mutations in TUBB4A
	- Defect in beta-tubulin, affecting microtubules
	- Probably affecting axonal transport
- Problem: MRI became a diagnostic tool, and a number of other clinical phenotypes were discovered
	- Ranging from very young children who only had hypomyelination, no other problems
	- to adults, who had completely different problems
- It turns out, that oligodendrocytes and astrocytes also provide food for axonal mitochondria (instead of food from cell body) -> could be a source of problems too
	- It turn out that neurons with H-ABC could not mature properly (due to this)
		- Shortened axons and reduced dendrites
		- All things that support full functionality of neurons! 
- H-ABC, with only hypomyelination (oligodendrocytic phenotype) caused by a transport defect (seen in Tairep rat)
