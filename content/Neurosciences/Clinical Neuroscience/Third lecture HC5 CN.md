## MS pathology and how we look at it
- Lesions, Diffuse WM injury, Cortical demyelination, Atrophy
- We use *MRI*
	- FLAIR /T2 
	- T1 (more for morphological sequence)
	- Diffusion weighted imaging
	- Functional MRI
- They all have different contrasts
	- In T1, white matter is white
		- Good contrast between grey and white matter
		- Lesions are black, very severe lesion that caused degeneration & axonal death
	- In T2, white matter is dark
		- Contrasts between grey and white is less
		- Lesions are white
	- In FLAIR (based on T2 with modification, try to remove the CSF signal)
		- You get hypointense CSF imaging
		- Best for lesion detection
		- You still see the lesions in white, but the rest is all very dark
### How MRI works
- MRI measures protons of hydrogen 
	- They have physical properties, and you can align all all of them with strong magnets
	- When you lie in a magnet (very strong)
	- With a helmet, you can launch a certain signal on it, and turn it
	- Then, when you remove that signal, it will return to align with the big magnet, which sends out some energy
	- This energy is measured by your helmet, and differences in these measurements are the different weights
	- The signal here is *direct*
### Diffusion weighted imaging
- This works a little bit different
	- You look at the movement of the water molecules
	- If you put water in an unconstrained place, the molecules will move randomly and not far away (brownian motion)
	- In the brain, water is constrained, axons are the constraints *of these water molecules*
		- Water will move in parallel to the axons, so you *indirectly* measure the axons 
		- If you make it a bit more complex (myelination, demyelination) also affect water movement
	- You can also look at the *amount of diffusion* -> amount of freedom water has to move
		- amount of diffusion + direction = tensor
		- remember, this is an *indirect method* (i think it might be on the test, he repeats this so much)
### Atrophy
- Damage to the brain, sometimes visible with the eye when looking at T1
- More often: use computers to see differences
- Only weak to modest correlation between atrophy and cognitive impairment in MS patients
### Advanced structural MRI
- DRI -> double inversion recovery
	- Also removes signal from white matter (FLAIR)
	- Allows you to detect 5 times more cortical lesions than a standard FLAIR image
	- 80% of cortical lesions seen through microscope are still missed by DIR
		- In postmortem data
	- DRI is also an *indirect measure* 
### fMRI principles
- When a brain area is activated, they require a lot of oxygen, so they first (and almost instantly) consume all oxygen around them
	- So, blood is rushed in 
	- fMRI looks at the changes in blood -> BOLD signal
		- Uses a physical property of the blood (oxygenated vs deoxygenated blood)
		- This state will change, and we can capture the signal of deoxygenation over oxygenated blood
		- for 10 minutes, they scan the brain every 3 seconds
	- You get for example different networks -> like default mode network
- fMRi for diagnosis:
	- there is a lot less activity of the affected regions
		- These regions cannot activate highly anymore, and the activity becomes so low you get memory problems
### Functional reorganization
- First, reorganization (hyperactivation in certain parts to counteract the loss of function of other parts) is seen, which allows for cognitive preservation
	- However, with more lesions, the cognitive impairment (hypoactivation) wins out and people become symptomatic
- Brain plasticity and fMRI
	- Plasticity relies on molecular and cellular mechanisms, to recruit parallel existing (or latent) connections
		- Functional MRI use the BOLD signal to provide an indirect measure of neural activity, powerful tool to measure brain plasticity in vivo
	- many fMRI studies use active paradigms to study the patterns of recruitment within differnet functional systems
		- People do a task, and so you measure what brain parts are used during the task
		- Issues with the technique: 
			- there are many cognitive domains, and a lot of different domains can be impaired
			- But if you do a task in the scanner, you are constrained to a single domain
				- so you cannot really measure a lot
		- Therefore, now we often do resting-state fMRi
			- resting state can also capture a lot of different networks
### Increased RS FC in MS patients
- Frustrating: there are opposite results all the time
	- Certain studies show increased connectivity in MS related to less disability
	- Others shows decreased RS FC was correlated in increase lesion volume
- Contradictory results within the same paradigm
	- Often mixed with different MS phenotypes, could play a role in this!
- Functional connectivity is not physically present, more an abstract term of
	- the degree to which different brain areas are connected to each other
- Results within MS:
	- More reorganization, less connection is some regions and more connections in others
	- reorganization is a good thing: it allows to compensate for lesions
		- seen in longitudinal studies
- Dynamic modular level aterations of structural-functional couplin in clinically isolated syndrome
	- So people probably use other pathways through different areas to compensate for problems in local pathways
	- MS leads to less dynamic brain function 
		- The other pathways are no longer available
