## Treatment of leukodystrophies
### Causal treatments
- Not available for most of the disorders
- If available, not for all patients, only for selected ones
### Challenges of developing causal treatments
- Ultrare disorders -> only like 10 in NL
- Lack of natural history data -> that is important, look at [[Third lecture HC2]]
- Mouse models available, but not always good models
	- Mice do not have *that much white matter*
- *multiple* mechanisms of pathology
	- If you have a problem with myelin, your axons become compromised slowly, which give different symptoms than the water homeostasis
- Individual/specific treatment approaches per disease
## Metachromatic leukodystrophy (MLD)
- first described in 1910
- Lysosomal storage disorder, autosomal recessive inheritance
- Low to no activity of arylsulfatase A (ASA)
- Develop demyelination
- Diagnosis:
	- Clinical MRI presentation
	- ASA activity in leukocytes
	- ARSA mutation analysis
	- Sulfatide excretion in urine
### MLD pathology
- Myelin eaten up by macrophages, but they get full of storage material, causes them to blow up
- Demyelination -> can still be reversed
	- But, if caught late, can cause axonal degeneration -> irreversible
### Clinical presentation MLD
- onset < 30 motnhs: late infantile form
	- Motor symptoms, rapid progression
- Onset 30 months - 16 years: juvenile form (early/late)
	- Rapid deterioration
	- cognitive symptoms
- Onset > 16 years: adult form
	- Slow onset
## Enzyme replacement therapy
- Clinicla trails already completed
- Problems: antibodies in a subset of patients
- Blood-brain barrier
	- Therefore, add it to the CSF directly -> had not a lot of effect
	- Idea is that the enzyme is taken up by the affected cells, but it still needs an post-translational modification 
- To be effective, enzymes must be delivered to the lysosomes of affected cells
	- To achieve this, post-translational modification mannose-6-phosphate is necessary (uses M6P receptors and transporters)
	- Enzyme produced by microglial cells (macrophages) is not taken up well as it does not have the correct modification
## Hematopoietic cell transplantation
- Transfuse blood stemcells from healthy patient into patient
	- Migration of healthy macrophages into the brain -> they become like microglia (macrophages)
	- Less problems, which allows oligodendrocytes to remyelinate the axons even
	- If you do this before the start of symptoms, you can prevent the disease from developing
- This is a *slow treatment* (like a year or half a year)
	- Earliest onset patients will not be helped by this. 
## Transplantation (HSCT)
- Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation
- Takes a long time, after 4,5 years white matter looks completely normal
- But, transplantation can still cause problems -> transplantation disease
	- Donor immune cells attack own body
## Gene therapy
- In vivo gene therapy
- It is attractive to treat directly the brain, with vector injection in the brain
	- Was tested in apes, it worked, but it was not effective at all in MLD patients
	- This is why this is no longer pursued 
- We now use ex vivo gene therapy
## Ex vivo gene therapy
- Correction of haemotopoietic stem cells
- You collect stem cells from the patients, and in vitro transduce iit with a lentivirus
	- Allows collected cells to overexpress ASA
	- have to use extensive chemotherapy to allow new stem cells to go to bone marrow
- After this, the newly generated cells migrate to the brain and do well there
- Conclusions:
	- Very effective in pre and early symptomatic patients with ML
	- registered as treatment for early forms of MLD in europe
	- Not effective for clearly symptomatic patients
		- and could even accelerate disease course
		- -> select patients carefully
- Its really fucking expensive -> 2,5 million per patient
