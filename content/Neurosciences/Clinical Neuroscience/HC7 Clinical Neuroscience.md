## Degeneration of substantia nigra

## CSTC circuits
- Cortico-striatal-thalamocortical circuits
- Via complicated feed-forward loop to thalamus
	- Connection between motor cortex and putamen
	- Orbitofrontal cortex and nucleus accumbens
	- Dorsolateral PFC to caudate
- 3-5 anatomically and functionally segregated parallel CSTC circuits
	- *Motor Circuits* 
		- SNc -> D1 + D2
		- D1 receptors become activated, D2 receptors become deactivated when dopamine binds
		- D1 -> GPi -> thalamus
		- D2 -> GPe -> STN -(inhibits)> GPi+SNr -> Thalamus
		- With less dopamine, there is more inhibition -> less movement
	- *Associative circuits*
	- *Limbic circuit*
	- Dopamine is the major neuromodulator of the parallel and segregated CSTC circuits
	- Dopamine degeneration leads to overactive 'indirect' pathway through tonic activity of D2R    -> Decreased cortical activity -> more inhibition
	- Dopamine replacement therapy and VDS correct the imbalance
	- Deep brain stimulation works through these pathways
## Pro-cons molecular imaging
- Pro:
	- High sensitivity for biomolecules
	- Quantifiable
- Con:
	- Radiation exposure
	- High cost (700 eu)
	- Low spatial resolution
	- Variability / fluctuations
	- Difficulty in designing tracers that can bind PD-relevant biomolecules AND cross BBB
## Detecting parkinsons with MRI
- With T1 weighted imaging, you cannot really make out whether a person has PD or not
- However MRI for differential diagnosis is good
	- For example, hummingbird syndrome
	- On the symptom level, there can be quite some overlap between Parkinsons and PSP or whatever
- On the individual level, they cannot be used to pinpoint parkinsons itself

## White matter microstructure
- Different *diffusivity* methods we can use
	- *Fractional Anisotropy Image*
		- FA = 1 when water can only diffuse in one way, = 0 when it can flow in whichever way it pleases (ventricles)
	- *Colour-Coded Tensor Image*
		- You look at the *cross-section* of fibre bundles,
			- Decreased bundle radius
			- Lower density bundle
- Quite clear and consistent evidence that there is decreased FA in *cingulate cortex, corpus callosum, substantia nigra* and *MORE* in *cortico-spinal tract* (to allow the minimum number of signals to still reach their goal, to compensate)
