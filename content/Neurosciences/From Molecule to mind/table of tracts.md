
| **Pathway**            | **Entry Point** (First-order neuron) | **Decussation Point** (Crossing)              | **Travel in the Spinal Cord**      | **End Point** (Final destination)              | **Lesion Symptoms**           |
|------------------------|--------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| **Anterolateral Tract (Spinothalamic)** | Dorsal root of the spinal cord                  | Immediately at the spinal cord level, typically within 1-2 segments after entry | Anterior and lateral columns (contralateral side) | Thalamus → Somatosensory cortex | **Contralateral** (pain and temperature loss below lesion) |
| **Dorsal Lemniscus (DCML)**    | Dorsal root of the spinal cord                  | Medulla oblongata (after synapse in nucleus gracilis/cuneatus) | Dorsal columns (ipsilateral until medulla)    | Thalamus → Somatosensory cortex | **Ipsilateral** (fine touch, proprioception loss below lesion) before decussation, **Contralateral** after decussation |
| **Corticospinal Tract**        | Primary motor cortex                          | Medulla oblongata (lateral corticospinal tract) | Lateral corticospinal: lateral column (contralateral); Anterior corticospinal: anterior column (ipsilateral) | Ventral horn of spinal cord → Muscles (via lower motor neurons) | **Contralateral** (muscle weakness or paralysis below lesion for lateral corticospinal); Anterior corticospinal lesions cause **minimal symptoms** due to bilateral control |

### Notes:
- **Anterolateral Tract**: This tract decussates immediately upon entering the spinal cord, so lesions usually produce **contralateral** sensory deficits (e.g., loss of pain and temperature sensation).
- **Dorsal Lemniscus (DCML)**: This pathway remains **ipsilateral** in the spinal cord and decussates only in the **medulla**. Therefore, lesions in the spinal cord cause **ipsilateral** symptoms (e.g., loss of fine touch, proprioception, vibration), while lesions above the decussation in the brainstem or higher lead to **contralateral** symptoms.
- **Corticospinal Tract**: The **lateral** corticospinal tract decussates in the **medulla** (causing contralateral motor symptoms for lesions below this point). The **anterior** corticospinal tract controls axial muscles and does not cross at the medulla, so its lesions tend to cause minimal symptoms due to **bilateral** control.
