## Before GWAS era
- We were mostly looking at genes through *candidate genes*
	- Select polymorphisms that we knew had an effect
	- Then basically look whether those had a measurable outcome
		- Same strategy as in GWAS, just on a smaller scale
- Issues with the candidate gene approach?
	- The genes really have really not held up
	- Confirmation bias
	- Lack of relevant knowledge
	- Polygenicity -> looking at a small number of genes is *not enough*
	- Low power (due to polygenicity)
	- Low power (statistical)
	- Missing correction for multiple testing
	- Analytical flexibility, HARK-ing, p-hacking
### P-values & correction for multiple testing
- This caused many multiple testing problems
	- *this gene* is influential on *this disease* under *these very specific circumstances*
		- For example, women who had depression from 14-16 who are now 32.

## Candidate vs GWAS
- Because of all these issues, the genetics field really did a 180 degree shift
	- We now do exploratory research, GWAS primarily
	- We are doing lasts of tests, but also correct for it
	- Do independent samples confirmation tests
- Still, some issues persist
	- If you do 1 million tests, you will find 50,000 significant hits!
	- Population stratification -> you control against a wrong population
	- over-interpreting false results
	- SNPs with a very low *minor allele frequency* have a higher chance to be significant
