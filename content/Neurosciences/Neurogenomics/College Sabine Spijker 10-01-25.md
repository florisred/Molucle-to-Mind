# Extracellular matrix in depression
Based on Riga et al., 2017

## Extracellular matrix
Extracellular matrix (based on sugars) changed in the model of depression in mice. The extracellular matrix did already come up during addiction. You can also make very beautiful pictures of the extracellular matrix.
What is it? Well, it is outside the cell. In the picture you have on the left side the neuron, and next to i many glycoproteins. There are various types of these proteins, but for now it suffices to know we have the Lectican family (Nn, Vn, Bn, An) which form a type of meshwork
Lectican is normally localized outside the cell. This has been known for ages. They described it almost 100 years ago. If you look at the 3D structure of it (yellow thing), you can already kind of see the neuron structure.  They are called perineuronal nets. 
But, then why are there holes in it? For communication. You can imagine that if you have more of this coating, you limit communication. 
These perineuronal nets are not only around the neuron, but also around the synapse. All of this has many functions, like mechanical support, migration, and many many more. One of the most known in neuroscience is upregulated at the moment to close the critical window of eye development. 
Visual plasticity in the cortex, you first get input from the left and right eye. In the cortex you have places where the left eye inputs on and the right eye inputs on, but in the end there will be one dominant eye. But, at some point this stops, and is fixed. And this fixing period is the end of the critical period, and a hallmark of that is upregulation of the extracellular matrix. 
## History of research on perineuronal nets
Has been going on for a long long time. But only since the last 20 years there has been research on lazy eye (extracellular matrix is involved). What is usually done with children who have a veyr dominant eye and one lazy eye (still in their critical period) you can stimulate by looking only with lazy eye the cortex, and cover the dominant eye. This only works in children before the critical period.
What researches did is take a look what is happening in the visual cortex. 
In the paper here they showed by using staining for aggregon (major component of perineuronal nets). You see these perineuronal nets become really nicely visible. 
## ECM and critical period plasticity
You have two different types of staining you can use. If you do monocular deprivation (close 1 eye) you will ensure that all the activity goes to the other eye (becomes more dominant). If you then open the eye, there will be almost no signal to the cortex. Then, what you can do is make sure that the extracellular matrix falls apart (with an enzyme). This way, you re-open the critical period. 
## Major Depressive Disorder
A lot of people focus on the mood part of depression, but if you look at the clinical features there is also a vast component of memory impairment. Spatial memory, working memory, etc.
In the literature, most people focus on sadness. But this is very hard to measure in animal models. However, concentration, working memory, navigation, all can be assessed. Also knowing that parts of hippocampal function are impaired was interesting.
Another thing that she was looking into was the fact you could have multiple states. You can have a depressive episode, go in remission, become close to normal, and then relapse into new periods. The only thing is that the cognitive function seems to be unrelated to the state of the depressive part. Another thing that you could say is that depression doesn't grow overnight, but instead needs to incubate. Sabine was more trained into long-term aspects due to the fact she had been working with addiction animal models. 
## Challenges in research on depression
- symptoms are difficult to reproduce in animal models
- neurobiological correlates are largely unknown
- antidepressants have low remission rates (30%) and a long therapeutic delay
- Depression is NOT stress, although stress is in important initiator. 
	- So, she wanted to look for a model of sustained depression in absence of acute stressors that induced depresive state. 
	- focus on cognitive deficits
## Disclaimer: 
She is an advocate for studying long term defects. This does not say all the other research is wrong, but more that she has a specific focus. She will also not go into what are valid models or tests.

## Social defeat-induced persistent stress (SPDS)
Focus on rats. You have a rat, huge rat, who are not very nice to other males. Especially when housed in a cage with a female (they have food, sex, and rock and roll). In the moment of social defeat we take out the female and put in an intruder rat on the other side of a barrier. Then they remove the barrier and there is a small fight phase. Then we stop it after 5-10 minutes. Rats don't normally do a lot of harm. But, one is bigger and one is younger and smaller. What they showed in various papers, a version of maintained depression for up to 6 months(both anhedonia and cognitive deficits) shows up. There is a treatment: paired housing. 
Treatment is often given during the stressor, which does not make sense to sabine, so they do it after. If you put it in a play cage for a few weeks every day it goes away. 
## Cognitive symptoms of the suicidal rats
Short term spacial memory is affected. What they do in such a test is put the animals in an arena with two objects, the rat can explore it, then take it out, and then remove one of the object. Then, the rat typically interacts mostly with the displaced object. Social defeat rats do not do fine in this experiment. This is measured a few months after their social defeat. If you treat them with IMI, the social defeated group restored. 
You can also do novel object recognition, but there is no change there. So only the spacial memory seems to be affected. 
## Changes in synaptic proteins?
They started to do proteomics analysis 25 years ago, but at that time they focussed on the dorsal hippocampus. A rat brain is much larger than a mouse brain, so they only did dorsal hippocampus. They did a trick with extracting synaptic molecules, and they did fingerprinting of the peptides. They found that of all the 519 proteins (laughable number), there was an overrepresentation of the extracellular matrix. 26 proteins were by Behavioral Therapy, and 18 proteins rescued by imi. 
## SDPS, focus on inhibitory input pyramidal neurons
- Frequency sIPSCs decreased
- decreased inhibitory input, but no amplitude effect. 
They found that the frequency of spontaneous IPSCs were diminished in the SDPS compared to controls. Then they asked, if depression related cognitive impairements and CA1 network changes could also be due to PNN-increase. 
They found that it restores spatial memory in behavior as well. 

## How to interpret data of other studies
Several other studies showed a downregulation, not an upregulation in the hippocampus of the extracellular matrix. But then they realized that if you take time into account, it makes sense. They did the same kind of test, and then the day after killed the animals and did western blots. In fact, they saw that behavior is affected shortly after the stressor, and at week 9. But, when looking at the extracellular matrix, you see something interesting. You see the downregulation in the hippocampus on the short term as well. So there then was a stress induced downregulation, and a depression caused upregulation.
