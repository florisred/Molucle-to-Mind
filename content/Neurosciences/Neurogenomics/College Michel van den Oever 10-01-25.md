## What are memory engrams?
It is actually not a new name, is was proposed in the early 20th century by Richard Semon. It basically means a learning induced physical change in the brain. The idea is that every learning experience leaves this trace, which is not  distributed over the whole brain, but that a sparse few neurons can trigger the memory. They are the same neurons that are activated when first learning it, and then can serve as (by triggering reminding cues, driving the same cells) to trigger the memory. This does not only happen in the hippocampus, but over the entire brain. 
So, what evidence do we have that this also happens in the human brain? Not only research on rodents but there is a nature study where they put electrodes in the brain for epilepsy. However, they also recorded activity for research purposes. They did this in the mediotemporal lobe, near the hippocampus, while they were shown pictures of celebrities. They found a neuron that specifically fires at different pictures of a specific picture of her, or when seeing her name. This was specific to this specific celebrity. 
*Is it the case that just this neuron encodes this specific celebrity?* No, there are also others, but this electrode was just placed near them. 
*Can a neuron participate with multiple engrams?* Yes

## Memory model: Contextual Fear conditioning
Mice were placed in a box and recieved a foot shock. A long time later they are put in the same place, and they get a freezing behavior. This is a natural repsonse to avoid predators. 
The uncoditioned stimulus is the foot shock, conditotnioned stimulus is the context, conditioned response is freezing. 
## How to label cells during training and see them 30 days later?
You need something to be permanently expressed. At the time there were only transient tags, that remain while the protein lasts (until its degraded.) So they used *viral-TRAP* (targeted recombination in activated populations). They used a Fos promotor, which drives *CreERT2*. Because of the ERT2 domain it stays in the plasma (while it would normally go in the nucleus). The same goes for the estrogen receptor domain (also needs to go in the nucleus). But, by adding 4TM (binding ERT2), Cre can go to the nucleus. Then, it un-inverts the flipped DREADD-receptor. (which is what Cre-Lox does).
*Why do the extra step of 4TM?* -> To be able to time it effectively! Now you can tag during a specific time. After, the tag (dreadd) is promoted by hSyn, a highly effective and permanent promotor. The Cre-Lox system will not ever invert again. You then  express both DREADD and mCherry, which functions as a permanent tag. The 4TM is active for about 4-5 hours.
*Why are we expressing DREADD and mCherry?* You can do it without, but we use it as a control.

## CREB and memory consolidation
It is known that CREB is very important for memory consolidation. You can impair memory by doing a knock out. It is important that it is phosphorylated (for transcription). There are mutant forms of CREB (synthetic) which cannot bind phosphor. Then you can block CREB mediated transcription. 
What happens with the behavior when we block CREB-mediation? No effect on recent memory, but severe impairment of remote memory.
