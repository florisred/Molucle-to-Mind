## Challenge to study human brain disorders
The final aim of the research is often to study the disorders of the human brain, in many different types of cases. We can recently also look at genetics, and we can look at the environment. So we also have to look at the interplay between environment and genetics, which cannot really be done in mouse models. GWAS is really good here for humans! After GWAS, you can look at biopsies and target specific genes. However, its still only a risk factor so its not that good. 
If you look at brain pathology, it is often the result of a big cascade. It could start with 1 process in 1 cell, but it results in a big cascade where problems arise in many cells with many different processes. So this is a big challenge. 
We also encounter problems when the in vivo situation is not fully understood yet. If we do not know the biological process, its hard to study
### Therapy targets
We are finally better at understanding and including glial cells in our research. That is good. 
Animal models are good, but often lack something. If you remove all white matter from mice, they are pretty fine for a long time. So therapies resulting from research on animal models often do not result in working clinic. 
### New representative models for developing human brain
We only have limited access to human brain tissue. We often use post-mortem, but we cant really look at that for developing diseases. So now we tend to use Human iPSC technology. These give us the opportunity to create stem cells from all living people IF CONSENT IS THERE. After this, we can create a model system for a specific individual. 
### Intrinsic vs cell interactive mechanisms
Cell-cell interactions are important to study. Especially glial cells! They are very necessary for neurons from iPSCs to develop normally. That is why we use co-culture systems. 
It is not like you have a good representation of a little human brain with iPSCs, you have a small structure that relates to a certain part of the brain. All of this is very new, which is why there are many different protocols between scientists, which can complicate matters.
It is important to keep in mind that the model you choose influences outcomes a lot. 
## Cortical neuron cultures
Sonic the Hedgehog is expressed in the ventral part of the brain, and binds to receptors there. This will turn on certain transcription factors based on the environment on the DNA. 
### Sandwich vs. continental co-cultures
This is where you grow to different types of cells on top of each other (like a sandwich) where they are not touching but still influence each other. Later you can separate these two from each other for research purposes. A continental co-culture is where you just grow them together. In both you are still able to create a very complex neural network. 
### Oligodendrocyte progenitors
You make Oligodendrocyte progenitors from floating embryoids, to different adherent stages. 
Oligodendrocytes really mostly grow if there is already a neural network there. So you never really have them *alone*. However, we can now kind of do that with overexpression. However, this is skipping certain stages. 
### Co-cultures and TSC
Axonal changes and hypertrophy have been repoted in brain tissue of TSC patients and TSC mouse models. We could measure these difference in the culture as soon as oligodendrocytes were added. This can have different explanations. One thing is that oligodendrocytes actually mature the neurons, so changes that only show in mature neurons are only visible then. 
All of this is to say, *it can be really important to include glia cells to get an accurate model!*
### Different types of models contrasted
An organoid model is a structure that resembles features in *early brain development*, like layering. This is often done to model early brains, or even evolution between species. The 3D spheroid models is of course three dimensional and come from early iPSCs. They push the cells together really hard, and put in a kind of gel. This gives a matrix to the cells. You could say youre skipping certain stages. You do not need to know the details, but you have to understand that there are many different protocols for creating these kinds of models. 
### Electrical activity measurements
You can measure the activity in the cells with an Multi-Electrode Array (MEA). With this, you have the ability to measure electrical over time. 
### Single cell analysis
You can now sequence any single different cell. This is more effective than bulk tissue analaysis, because that fails to be informative due to the large number of different cell types and states. Single cell profiling will give a lot more specific information. Especially, if you do many different single cell analyses, which is now being done in the US.
