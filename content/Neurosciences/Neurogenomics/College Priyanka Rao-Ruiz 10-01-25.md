## Memory processing is dynamic
### Fear conditioning, memory processes
Firstly, when putting a mouse in the cage after learning, it will have *retrieval*. What we know can happen during the retrieval session it can become an unstable form. In this period, you can modify it a little bit. It has to be destabilized later, called reconsolidation. It is not exactly the same as first consolidation. This is triggered if you do a *short retrieval*. If you do a long retrieval, so put the animal for longer in the cage than 3 minutes, it realizes that it is not that scary actually, and *modifies the memory*. How do we know its a new form of learning and not just a deletion? Because you can have renewal: re-emergence of fear with a change of context from the one in which extinction occurred. Reinstatement: re-emergence of elevated fear responding with un-signaled US presentation. Mostly relevant for auditory cues. 
### Reconsolidation
Reconsolidation is dependent on protein synthesis (which takes about 6 hours). We know this because if we block protein synthesis outside the 6 hour time window, you do not affect the memory. Reconsolidation is fundamental but not ubiquitous. We do not know exactly what triggers it, and even though the name is re-consolidation, it is different to the original consolidation process. 
Why would we need to reconsolidate certain memories? Well, this way you can change it. You can change the strength, or the content of the memory. You can imagine this is important in nature, it is good to update your knowledge. It is adaptive!
For fear conditioning, could do a 'safe' or 'non-threatening' re-write of the memory.

### Reconsolidation: synaptic protein expression analysis
The three most common Glutamate receptors (GluA1,2,3) -> acute downregulation. They then had to show if it really happened because of retrieval. Then there is no regulation of the AMPA receptor. 
Then they wanted to know if it had to do with retrieval of in a novel context. Also no downregulation. They found this happens in a biphasic wave. First you have synaptic weakening, and then restabalization. First fewer, then more AMPA receptors. The decay is also different, due to a difference in distribution of the GluA1-GluA2-GluA3 receptors. 
### Blocking endocytosis of AMPARs
Then, they very specifically wanted to stop the endocytosis of the AMPA receptors. Luckily, you can make synthetic peptides, and can enter the cell membrane due to TAT. Then, all the binding partners will all bind to peptides instead of the AMPARs, which then stops endocytosis. 
First they checked if the peptides were actually doing what they were supposed to be doing. Yes. What was very interesting as well: It also stopped the subsequent upregulation after 7 hours. They tested this with respect to molecules, but it also stopped the 'switch' at 7 hours in distribution.
Effect on memory strength: Distrupting AMPAR endocytosis results in enhanced fear memory. So, Hippocampal plasticity strength blocks the fear of fear, stops is from becoming worse and worse and worse.
